01 · The course

A tour of the whole city, closed to traffic

Start and finish at Rådhusparken. In between: Frederiksbjerg, the river, the Latin Quarter, out to the harbour front at Aarhus Ø, and down the coast road to Marselisborg.

    04 · Where the river narrows

    Pressure points on the course

    The route is coloured by how many runners share each stretch of road. Red means a crowd.

    Computing…

    Runner positions are modelled: net time per finisher, plus start delays and pacing curves from a sample of runners' timing-mat splits (start, 1, 5, 10, 15, 20 km). It shows where to look, not exact counts.

    05 · Running

    How Aarhus paces a half marathon

    Median pace over each part of the course, by finish group. Every group fades after 15 km, the slower groups the most, and every group finds a finishing kick for the last kilometre into Rådhusparken.

    Lights are now coloured by finish time: fast, middle, steady.

    08 · Why this page exists

    What a little data and AI can do for Aarhus Motion

    I built this as part of my application to continue as an Aarhus Motion ambassador, using only the public results, OpenStreetMap and a GPS track of the course. A few things it could become:

    • A race recap page after every event: the morning replayed, with a shareable card for every runner.
    • A planning view for the organisers: test start waves, route changes or new aid stations against last year's crowd before race day.
    • Social clips: short flyovers of the course and the field for Instagram and the newsletter.
    • Community stories: which clubs and companies grow, who comes back every year, from the winter runs to the City Half.

    Use the time slider in the corner to explore the morning yourself. Drag to look around.
