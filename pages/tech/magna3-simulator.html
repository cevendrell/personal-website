What's inside

Wet-rotor PM motor

The rotor carries permanent magnets and spins inside a thin stainless rotor can, in the heating water itself. No shaft seal, so nothing can leak. The water also cools and lubricates the bearings, and the pump can run against closed valves for days.

Composite impeller and volute

Water rises from the lower flange, loops behind the pump into the eye of the impeller, is flung outward by the blades and leaves through the upper flange. Pressure builds along the way: the particles turn from blue to orange.

AUTOADAPT

The factory setting. It's proportional pressure from a fixed 1.5 m base, starting at about 55% of max head. When the pump finds itself on its max curve at a lower head, it picks a lower curve on its own. Here that learning is sped up from hours to seconds.

Limits you can hit

Between about 5 and 20 m³/h the pump reaches its 333 W input limit before top speed. FLOW LIMIT caps the flow between 25% and 90% of max. Hot water needs inlet pressure: 0.7 bar at 110 °C to avoid cavitation.

Built with the teardown method: real numbers first, one physics function that drives every part and gauge, and an idle auto tour that takes over after 25 seconds.

The outer shell is Grundfos's own CAD model of product 97924259 (housing, motor head and control box), turned into a web mesh and recoloured to match the product photo. The pump curves are fitted to the official performance chart. Control behaviour, inlet pressures and limits follow the MAGNA3 installation and operating instructions. The internals (impeller, rotor, can, stator) aren't in the public CAD, so they're drawn in code to plausible proportions. This is an illustration, not an engineering tool.
