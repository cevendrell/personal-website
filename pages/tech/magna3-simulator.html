MAGNA3 circulator, taken apart

A working 3D model of a Grundfos MAGNA3 32-120 F heating pump, built to the dimensions of Grundfos's drawing and CAD model and driven by its official pump curves. Change the control mode, close the valves, pull the pump apart or cut it open. Speed, flow, head and power all come from one physics model, so the impeller, the water, the chart and the pump's display always agree.

What's inside

Wet-rotor PM motor

The rotor carries permanent magnets and spins inside a thin stainless rotor can, in the heating water itself. No shaft seal, so nothing can leak. The water also cools and lubricates the bearings.

Composite impeller and volute

Water rises from the lower flange, loops behind the pump into the eye of the impeller, is flung outward by the blades and leaves through the upper flange. Pressure builds along the way: the particles turn from blue to orange.

Sensor-led control

A built-in sensor reads differential pressure and temperature. The frequency converter in the control box changes speed (up to 4,800 rpm) to hold the chosen curve. Proportional pressure lowers the head as the valves close.

Power limit

Between about 5 and 20 m³/h the pump reaches its 333 W input limit before top speed, which gives the official curve its straight sloping edge. Pick constant curve at 100% and open the valves to watch the speed drop back.

Built with the teardown method: real numbers first, one physics function that drives every part and gauge, all geometry generated in code, and an idle auto tour that takes over after 25 seconds.

The outside dimensions come from the Grundfos dimension drawing and CAD model of product 97924259. They give the S-shaped housing, the flanges, the finned motor head and the L-shaped control box. The pump curves are fitted to the official Grundfos performance chart. The internals (impeller, rotor, can, stator) aren't in the public CAD, so they're drawn to plausible proportions. This is an illustration, not an engineering tool.
